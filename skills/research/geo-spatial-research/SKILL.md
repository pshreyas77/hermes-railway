# Geo-Spatial Research

This skill governs the process of researching geographic phenomena (e.g., flooding, pollution, urban development) across large administrative regions like states or provinces.

## Workflow

1.  **Initial Sweep**: Identify the primary areas of concern. Use broad queries to find reports, news summaries, or official government lists.
2.  **Categorization**: Group findings by the *nature* of the problem*. For example, in flood research, distinguish between urban drainage failure, riverine overflow, and flash floods.
3.  **Exhaustive Validation**: When a user asks "Does this happen in ALL X?", do not assume based on a few examples. Explicitly verify the remaining areas.
4.  **Comparative Analysis**: Create a matrix or table comparing different regions to show the nuance in how the problem manifests (Scale, Cause, Impact).
5.  **Final Mapping**: Provide a comprehensive list covering all administrative units (States, UTs, Provinces) to ensure no gaps in the answer.

## Pitfalls & Lessons

- **The "Representative Sample" Trap**: Avoid claiming a problem is nationwide just because it's common in major cities. Actively search for the "outlier" regions (e.g., desert states or mountain ranges) to see how they experience the phenomenon.
- **Terminology Nuance**: Search for multiple terms. "Urban Flooding" is different from "Waterlogging" and "Flash Floods." Using only one term may miss half the data.
- **Dynamic Data**: For environmental issues, always specify the time range (e.g., "last 2 years") as infrastructure and weather patterns shift.

## Verification Steps
- Cross-reference city-level data with state-level reports.
- Ensure all administrative entities of the target country are accounted for in the final response.
